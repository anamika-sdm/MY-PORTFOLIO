About

A designer by training.
A question-collector by habit.
Still learning what to make of it all.

I have a habit of looking twice. At the way people behave when nobody tells them what to do. At the little decisions that make a brand feel like itself. At the gap between what something promises and what it actually gives.

Somewhere between visual communication and strategic design, I found a question I keep returning to: what if the interesting part is not what we see, but what made it that way?

I. / The things I wonder about

The obvious is a good place to start.
Not always a good place to stop.

A few thoughts from the margins of my notebook.

NOTE 01 — OBSERVE

Small things, long shadows.

A queue that bends without instruction.
A room that makes everyone whisper.
A brand recognised before its name is read.

I collect these small hows and whys — little clues to the systems we move through without noticing.

NOTE 02 — QUESTION

There is usually another why.

Who decided this was the way?
Who does it work for?
What have we mistaken for normal?

I trust a useful question more than a quick answer. Certainty is tidy; reality, rather inconveniently, is not.

“Perhaps design begins where the first explanation ends.”

I studied Fashion Communication at NIFT Delhi, where I began thinking about how stories take shape through images, words and identity. Working with a brand brought those questions into practice: how do all the little pieces come together to feel like one recognisable voice?

At NID Gandhinagar, my questions have widened. I am exploring human behaviour, services and systems — the things that shape an experience long before anyone calls it design.

Different scales, perhaps. The same instinct: look closer, question the given, try another way.

II. / A field guide to my head

Three recurring habits.

Not quite a method. More like the route my mind takes when left to wander.

i. Notice

The detail that doesn't quite belong. The pause before an answer. The unofficial way people use a space. Sometimes the footnote is the story.

ii. Unpick

Pull at the thread. Test the assumption. Ask the awkward question gently — or not so gently, depending on the assumption.

iii. Try

Make a version. Follow the strange idea for a while. Let the first attempt be a question, not a verdict. See what happens.
