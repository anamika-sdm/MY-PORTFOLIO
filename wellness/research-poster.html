The core problem

“The challenge isn’t availability — it’s the missing user journey.”

The problem lies in discovery & decision-making.

India lacks

  • Mandatory national accreditation
  • Structured pre-visit consultation
  • Unified digital health record

Global benchmarking

Compared across 10 countries, India is the only one lacking all three system components, despite a $15B+ market.

Bali · Thailand · Sri Lanka · Germany · Switzerland · UAE · USA · UK · Singapore

Medical tourism: structured, outcome-clear, trust-driven.
Wellness tourism: fragmented, unclear journey, low trust signals.

Insights + solutions

From patterns to design

01

Ayurveda as aftercare, not prevention

Adopted after other treatments fail; use peaks at 40–49, not embedded in prevention.

02

Packaged wellness ≠ personalised care

Commercial scale vs. diagnosis-led care — a mismatch that confuses consumers.

03

Complex, inconsistent, unverifiable

No credible standard to check; users freeze or choose purely on aesthetics.

04

Clinical credibility vs. commercial appeal

Hospitals for credibility, resorts for tourists — the trust gap deepens.

05

Wellness = luxury, not infrastructure

Top of the lifestyle hierarchy — conditional and aspirational, not foundational.

Solution framework

Four design directions

Standardise

NABH reform · Tiered certification · Patient journey protocol · Wellness definition framework

Connect

National wellness directory · OTA + e-AYUSH integration · Planner network

Communicate

Leverage

Brand India Wellness · Ministry of Tourism alignment · Corporate wellness programmes
