koo kie

Because drink needs a cookie.

Brand identityProduct managementPackagingGo-to-market

Pick your drink. We’ll pick the cookie.

“Redefining indulgence, one perfect sip-and-bite pairing at a time.”

India’s premium cookie shelf is crowded with gooey, stuffed, New York–style cookies — but every one of them is built to be eaten on its own. Koo & Kie starts from the cup instead. Each cookie is designed around a drink: chai, coffee, green tea or herbal tea.

The recipes stay thick, soft and flavour-packed, but use multigrain atta, jaggery and nuts, and keep sweetness low so the cookie lifts the drink rather than drowning it.

The projectA new D2C and café cookie brand, from market research to launch plan

A big market, moving upmarket

Biscuits are an everyday staple in India. The growth, though, is in premium and better-for-you — exactly the gap between a ₹10 packet and a ₹300 artisanal cookie.

What’s shifting

Gourmet goes mainstream

Urban millennials spend more on gourmet and artisanal cookies (IBEF).

New occasions

Cookies are now bought for gifting, on-the-go breakfast and office snacking (IBEF, TechSci).

Festive spikes

Premium cookie sales jump around Diwali and Christmas (TechSci).

Portable formats

Around half of biscuit packaging is pouches and packets built for portability (IMARC).

Beyond the metros

40% of affluent Indians live outside major metros, so premium taste is spreading (Fortune).

The big three, and what they own

Pioneered India’s centre-filled cookie segment in 2010. Holds about 22% of the ₹3,067 crore premium cookie market and crossed ₹1,000 crore in annual sales by 2025.
Leads the health biscuit segment with around 70% of premium digestives and over ₹1,000 crore in revenue (FY22). Britannia holds about 38% of the organised biscuit market.
Launched in India in 2011. Strong recognition but a modest local share — about 10% of the premium cream-biscuit segment, roughly ₹270–280 crore in revenue.

Mass brands own either indulgence or health. Nobody owns the moment the cookie is actually eaten — with a drink.

Who it’s for, and how it stands out

The coffee connoisseur

Primary D2C buyer. Values authenticity and sees the pairing as an extension of their brewing ritual. Swayed by expert reviews.

The urban entertainer

High order value. Uses food to host and gift, drawn by packaging and flavour stories.

The mindful indulger

Secondary volume. Wants control — less sweet, high fibre, one portion at a time.

Specialty coffee chains

Core B2B in Tier-1 metros. Needs centralised logistics and a high-margin SKU.

Premium ancillary retail

Co-working spaces, boutique hotels and food halls that need individual, tamper-proof packs.

Demographically: 18–40 years old, slightly female-skewed (55:45), ₹50,000+ monthly income — students, corporate professionals, café-goers and fitness-conscious buyers in urban India.

D2C: The Ritualist

Urban millennials, 30–45, for whom a daily specialty coffee or cold brew is non-negotiable.

Need: a cookie that makes the drink better. They drive subscriptions for the coffee and cold brew lines.

B2B: The Specialty Chain Partner

Operations and procurement heads at chains like Blue Tokai and Subko.

Need: a zero-headache supplier for a unique branded SKU with 50%+ margin. They drive national contracts.

Koo & Kie is the single-portion, chunky New York–style cookie designed to complete every sip — from specialty coffee to fine wine.

High indulgence, high function

Chunky, gooey, XL cookies — with flavours engineered for a specific drink.

Not too sweet

Built for the Indian palate that finds most imported cookies cloying.

Eight cookies, three cups

The range is organised by drink, not by flavour, so customers never have to guess which cookie goes best.

The 4 Ps

Product

  • Better-for-you cookies made to go with tea, coffee and other drinks
  • Multigrain atta, whole wheat, jaggery and nuts
  • Flavours that support the drink instead of overpowering it
  • Sleek, recyclable packs

Price

  • ₹65 a cookie — accessible premium
  • Pack of 4 at ₹240
  • 8–10% D2C discount on repeat orders
  • Justified by ragi, whole wheat, dark chocolate and natural sweeteners

Place

  • D2C website and hyperlocal delivery, for storytelling and subscriptions
  • Café and corporate tie-ups for impulse buys
  • Premium supermarkets and health stores in metros
  • Kiosks and pop-ups for tasting

Promotion

  • From snack to perfect pairing, with better-for-you ingredients
  • Reels and Shorts showing texture, dunking and unboxing
  • Café table tents suggesting pairings
  • Pairing sessions with coffee and tea brands
